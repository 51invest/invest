Building an Investment Research Process

Turn an opinion into a traceable, falsifiable research file: state the question and falsification conditions, then check the 10-K, 8-K and EDGAR source documents; all example numbers are hypothetical.

ModuleINV-M01 · 目标与投资流程
LevelFoundation
Read4 min · 14 sections

Problem and output

With limited time, research must turn scattered facts into a record another person can reproduce and you can overturn yourself. Inputs are objectives and constraints, public filings and a time budget. The output is a research file holding the question, assumptions, evidence references, falsification conditions, update triggers and a separated conclusion. It does not guarantee a correct call, only a traceable one.

Inputs and data requirements

  • objectives, horizon and constraints (see INV-001), which set depth
  • filings: Form 10-K annual report, Form 10-Q quarterly report, Form 8-K current report, and “/A” amendments
  • audited financial statements and Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)
  • industry and macro data with definitions and release dates
  • valuation inputs and their sources
  • time budget and review date

Assumptions

The example assumes 8 hours a month for one company, free public filings only, a 12-month horizon, scenario returns of +20%, +3% and -35% as teaching assumptions, and thresholds (revenue growth 5%, net debt/EBITDA 3.0 times) that illustrate rather than set an industry standard.

Steps

  1. Write the question and the decision. State what must be answered and which decision changes.
  2. Build the annual-report baseline. Follow the 10-K structure: Business, Risk Factors for the significant risks disclosed (the page reads "includes information about significant risks that the company faces, generally listed in order of importance"), Selected Financial Data ("provides certain financial information about the company for the last five years") and MD&A for trends and management's explanation, then the audited financial statements [1].
  3. Set event monitoring. Most 8-K items are due within four business days of the triggering event, catching material changes before the next periodic report - the page states "Companies must file 8-Ks promptly, rather than waiting until their next periodic report" and that companies must make most 8-K disclosures "within four business days of the triggering event and in some cases even earlier" [2].
  4. Go back to source documents. Search EDGAR by form type ("EDGAR presents search results in chronological order and identifies the filings by form types"), download filings, and separate 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K and "/A" amendments ("Filings with “/A” appended to the form type code indicate an amendment") instead of relying on summaries [3].
  5. Write assumptions and falsification conditions. Give each conclusion an observable condition that would overturn it, such as revenue growth below 5% for two quarters or negative operating cash flow for two quarters.
  6. Run scenarios and valuation. Use at least three scenarios and label the source and retrieval date of every key input.
  7. Separate the conclusion. Record facts, inferences and opinions in separate sections, each pointing to a page or link.
  8. Review quarterly. Compare forecasts with outcomes, append records, and never rewrite old ones.

Example

Suppose a file records a 12-month horizon, scenario returns of +20%, +3% and -35%, and a falsification condition of “revenue growth below 5% or operating cash flow negative for two quarters.” Three months later an 8-K on an auditor change triggers a review, which only appends records and marks the conclusion “unverified.” Without a pre-written condition, the change would be dismissed as noise.

Interpreting the result

The process buys traceability and falsifiability, not accuracy. In this article's view, the order of the 10-K can serve as a reading method: business and risks first, then numbers, then management's explanation [1]. Periodic reports lag, and the 8-K adds timely disclosure of material events [2]; the EDGAR form type and “/A” marker determine whether the version in hand is current [3]. Separating facts, inferences and opinions shows which layer failed at review.

Failure conditions

  • reading only press releases or commentary instead of the filing
  • missing an “/A” amendment, or treating a superseded figure as final
  • taking the MD&A narrative as fact rather than management's explanation
  • no falsification condition, so the conclusion drifts with price and story
  • covering too many names for the time budget and leaving files unfinished
  • rewriting past records at review, which hides the error
  • using one document to support opposite conclusions

Verification

  1. Ask a second person to recompute the key figures from the file alone.
  2. Compare the prior conclusion with the outcome and record the difference.
  3. Sample three conclusions and confirm each points to a filing and section.
  4. Check data versions and retrieval dates.

Reproduction

The template records the subject and date, the question, the linked decision, assumptions, an evidence list (form type, filing date, link), falsification conditions and thresholds, scenarios, the separated conclusion and the next review date. Another person should be able to rebuild the reasoning from that list.

Exercises

  1. Locating a section. Which part of a 10-K most directly lists the significant risks the company identifies?
  2. Timing. How does the 8-K differ from the 10-Q in disclosure timing?
  3. Falsification. Why must each conclusion carry an observable condition that would overturn it?
Suggested answers
  1. Risk Factors, generally listed in order of importance.
  2. An 8-K covers material events, and most items are due within four business days, sometimes sooner; a 10-Q is the quarterly periodic report.
  3. It turns an opinion into a testable hypothesis and lets a review locate whether the assumption, evidence or reasoning failed.
